# Licensing

LibraOS is free for personal use, for development, and for small
organizations. Other commercial use requires a paid licence. The terms are the
[LIBRAOS LICENSE](https://github.com/libraos/libraos/blob/main/LICENSE)
distributed with each release; this page summarizes them and does not replace
them. Where the two differ, the LICENSE governs.

## Which licence applies

| You are | Free licence | What it covers | Production use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A person using LibraOS for yourself | Personal Use (§2a) | Learning, experiments, hobby and unpaid personal projects, on infrastructure you control | Not applicable |
| A developer building on LibraOS, including for an employer or client | Developer License (§3) | Non-production development, testing and evaluation, for one named person with a LibraOS account | No |
| An organization deciding whether to buy | Evaluation Use (§2b) | Installing, configuring, testing and benchmarking, up to 90 days per organization | No |
| A small organization: revenue plus funding under US$200,000 over the last 12 months | Small Organization License (§4) | Any use, including commercial and production | Yes |
| A school or registered non-profit, for teaching or non-commercial research | §2c | Teaching, coursework and non-commercial academic research not funded by a commercial sponsor | Not applicable |
| Anyone else using it in or for an organization | Commercial licence (§5) | Set by a written agreement | As agreed |

Use in or for an organization counts as commercial use even when it is
internal or earns nothing. Every free licence allows any number of instances.
The free licences grant no right to support, updates or a service level;
programme benefits offered to developers, such as inference credits, are not
rights under the LICENSE and can change (§2, §3).

Some things need a separate written agreement whatever licence you hold (§6):
reselling or redistributing LibraOS, embedding it in another product, offering
it as a hosted service to others, and building a competing product.

## The licence record in the Developer Console

The [Developer Console](https://console.libraos.com) keeps a record of the
licence an account has claimed: the edition, the terms version accepted, who
accepted them, when, and until when.

- **It is a record, not an activation.** There is nothing to download,
  install or enter. The runtime does not check the record, contact LibraOS to
  validate it, or unlock features with it. Your rights come from the LICENSE.
- **What the Console records today:** claiming the free licence records a
  90-day organization evaluation, for development and evaluation, without
  production use. It is the narrower of the grants the LICENSE offers, and it
  is recorded the same way for every account for now. Claiming again does not
  renew an expired evaluation.
- **Not in the Console yet:** accepting the Developer License terms for a
  named person (§3), and certifying a Small Organization (§4), which the
  LICENSE asks for in an account and renews every 12 months. Until the Console
  offers them, write to the licensing contact below.

Commercial and enterprise licences are recorded in the Console by our team
once the agreement is in place.

## Commercial licences

Production use by an organization that is not a Small Organization needs a
commercial licence (§5). It is agreed in writing, and sets the fees, the scale
of deployment and the support terms. A signed agreement takes precedence over
the LICENSE for what it covers (§14).

Licensing enquiries: [contact@libraos.com](mailto:contact@libraos.com). See also
[pricing](https://libraos.com/pricing/).

## Other things to know

- **Changes to the terms** apply only to releases published on or after the
  date they take effect. A release you already have keeps the terms it shipped
  with (§7h).
- **Verification:** the LICENSE lets Nebula Nova Inc. ask for reasonable
  evidence that you qualify for the licence you rely on (§7b).
- **Your data:** content you process with LibraOS and the output you generate
  remain yours (§10).
- **Third-party components** keep their own licences (§9). The documentation
  and blog are CC BY 4.0, and the documentation site's code is MIT.
